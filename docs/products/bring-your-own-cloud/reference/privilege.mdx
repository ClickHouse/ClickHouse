This page gives a summary of the identities that ClickHouse BYOC uses in your cloud account. For the permissions, scope, and purpose of each identity, see the page for your cloud:

- [BYOC privilege on AWS](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/privilege-aws)
- [BYOC privilege on GCP](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/privilege-gcp)
- [BYOC privilege on Azure](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/privilege-azure)

## AWS IAM roles {#aws-iam-roles}

### Bootstrap IAM role {#bootstrap-iam-role}

The bootstrap IAM role, `ClickHouseManagementRole`, has the following permissions:

- **EC2 and VPC operations**: Required for setting up VPC and EKS clusters.
- **S3 operations (e.g., `s3:CreateBucket`)**: Needed to create buckets for ClickHouse BYOC storage.
- **IAM operations (e.g., `iam:CreatePolicy`)**: Needed for controllers to create additional roles (see the next section for details).
- **EKS operations**: Write operations are limited to resources with names starting with the `clickhouse-cloud` prefix.

### Additional IAM roles created by the controller {#additional-iam-roles-created-by-the-controller}

In addition to the `ClickHouseManagementRole`, the controller creates several additional roles for each BYOC infrastructure:

- **Data plane management**: The ClickHouse management service assumes it, with your external ID, for day-to-day management of your ClickHouse services.
- **EKS cluster and node roles**: Used by EKS, the system nodes, and the Karpenter nodes.
- **Cluster controllers**: Karpenter, Cluster Autoscaler, the AWS Load Balancer Controller, and the EBS CSI driver.
- **ClickHouse server identity**: Reads and writes table data and backups in the BYOC buckets.
- **Monitoring storage (Thanos)**: Reads and writes metrics in the BYOC monitoring bucket.
- **Telemetry roles**: Send infrastructure health, usage data for billing, and Kubernetes metrics for autoscaling to ClickHouse. They have no other permissions.

For the permissions and scope of each policy and role, see [BYOC privilege on AWS](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/privilege-aws).

## GCP service accounts {#gcp-service-accounts}

### Bootstrap service account {#bootstrap-service-account}

The bootstrap service account, `clickhouse-management`, is granted project-scoped custom roles with the following permissions:

- **Common**: Baseline read and identity permissions.
- **VPC**: Manage the VPC, subnets, routing, and Private Service Connect attachments that host your BYOC infrastructure.
- **Cluster**: Manages GKE clusters and in-cluster resources.
- **Storage**: Used to manage Cloud Storage buckets used for ClickHouse backups, shared state, and monitoring data.
- **IAM Role**: Manages service accounts and custom roles inside the project. This role does not grant the ability to create service account keys, bind organization policies, or touch any resources in other projects.

### Additional service accounts created by the controller {#additional-service-accounts-created-by-the-controller}

In addition to the `clickhouse-management` service account, the controller creates several additional service accounts for each BYOC infrastructure. Each has a narrow, single-purpose permission set:

- **Data plane management identity**: The ClickHouse management service impersonates it for day-to-day management of your ClickHouse services, such as buckets, access for the server pods, and Private Service Connect.
- **GKE node identity**: Attached to all GKE nodes. Writes logs and metrics, and pulls container images.
- **ClickHouse server identity**: Reads and writes table data and backups in the buckets of each ClickHouse service.
- **Monitoring identity**: Reads and writes metrics in the BYOC monitoring bucket.
- **Telemetry identities**: Send usage data for billing and Kubernetes metrics for autoscaling to ClickHouse. They have no permissions in your project.
- **TDE delegate** (only if TDE is on): Encrypts and decrypts the TDE data keys with your KMS key.

For the permissions and scope of each role and service account, see [BYOC privilege on GCP](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/privilege-gcp).

The state exporter, which publishes service and backup status events to ClickHouse Cloud, is deliberately not in this list: on GCP its publishing identity is a ClickHouse-owned service account in a ClickHouse-owned project, not one created in yours. What is created in your project is a workload identity binding that lets the in-cluster `state-exporter` service account impersonate it. No key material is involved, and the identity that publishes to Pub/Sub holds no permissions in your project.

## Azure roles and identities {#azure-roles-and-identities}

### Onboarding service principal {#onboarding-service-principal}

Onboarding with the [Terraform module for Azure](https://github.com/ClickHouse/terraform-byoc-onboarding/tree/main/modules/azure) creates a service principal in your tenant for the ClickHouse BYOC multi-tenant application. The service principal gets the custom role `ClickHouse BYOC Provisioner` on the subscription, with the following permissions:

- **Networking**: Manage the VNet, subnets, public IPs, NAT gateways, network security groups, and Private Link services that host your BYOC infrastructure.
- **Cluster**: Manage AKS clusters and node pools.
- **Storage**: Manage the storage accounts and blob containers used for ClickHouse data, backups, and monitoring data. No deletion of storage accounts or containers.
- **Identity**: Manage user-assigned managed identities and their federated identity credentials.
- **Authorization**: Manage custom role definitions and role assignments.

All permissions are limited to the subscription. The role cannot change resources in other subscriptions or tenants. If you [bring your own IAM](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/onboarding/customization-azure#customer-managed-iam-azure), most permissions are limited to the resource groups of each BYOC infrastructure.

### Additional managed identities created by the controller {#additional-managed-identities-created-by-the-controller}

In addition to the onboarding service principal, the controller creates several user-assigned managed identities for each BYOC infrastructure. All ClickHouse services on the infrastructure share them. Each one gets tokens through a federated identity credential for a Kubernetes service account (workload identity):

- **Data plane management identity**: The ClickHouse management service uses it for day-to-day management of your ClickHouse services, such as storage accounts, access for the server pods, and private endpoint connections.
- **ClickHouse server identity**: Reads and writes table data and backups in the storage accounts of the ClickHouse services.
- **Monitoring identity**: Reads and writes metrics for Prometheus and Thanos.
- **TDE delegate** (only if TDE is on): Encrypts and decrypts the TDE data keys with the key in your key vault.

The controller also creates an Entra application and a service principal for the AKS cluster, with `Network Contributor` on the default resource group of the infrastructure. The telemetry identities, which send usage data for billing and Kubernetes metrics for autoscaling, are in a ClickHouse subscription. They have no permissions in your subscription.

For the permissions and scope of each role and managed identity, see [BYOC privilege on Azure](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/privilege-azure).

As on GCP, the state exporter is deliberately absent from this list: its publishing identity is a ClickHouse-owned user-assigned managed identity in a ClickHouse-owned subscription. A federated identity credential on that identity lets the in-cluster `state-exporter` service account get tokens for it, with no secrets exchanged. The identity that publishes to Service Bus holds no permissions in your subscription.

<Note>
This differs from AWS, where the publishing role is created in your own account and assumes a ClickHouse-side role to reach the queue. On GCP and Azure the publishing identity sits entirely on the ClickHouse side and your cluster only federates into it, so there is no additional in-account permission for you to audit for this flow. The egress itself is inventoried in [network boundaries](/products/bring-your-own-cloud/reference/network-security#outbound-connections).
</Note>
